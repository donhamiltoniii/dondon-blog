# The Base Layer System

A cheap, whole-food way to eat on autopilot. It has three parts, each prepped in bulk on its own schedule, and you put them together at every meal. On top of that, seasonal vegetables from the market get one or two simple treatments. Change the parts when you get bored. The structure stays the same.

This is **component cooking**: you prep building blocks, not recipes.

**Daily targets:** ~3000 cal · 220g protein · 40g+ fiber. Nothing else is tracked.

---

## The Three Parts

| Part                 | What it does                               | Batch size          | Prep cadence                                      | Keeps                        |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Base**             | Fiber, carbs, about a third of the protein | 6 servings          | Every 3 days                                      | 4–5 days in the fridge       |
| **Protein**          | Most of the protein                        | ~4 lb raw           | Every 3 days                                      | 3–4 days in the fridge       |
| **Ferment**          | Acid, crunch, live cultures                | 1 half-gallon jar   | Start a new one when the current jar is half gone | Months in the fridge         |
| **Market additions** | Variety, seasonality, micronutrients       | Whatever you bought | Weekly market run                                 | 3–5 days (preserved: weeks+) |

**A bowl:** 1 serving of base, ~225g cooked protein, a big scoop of ferment, and whatever market addition you have on hand. Eat it hot or cold. Add the ferment last, and never heat it.

**Two bowls a day** is about 2000 cal and 200g protein. Breakfast and snacks cover the rest.

## 1. The Base

### How to build it

Every batch uses the same template:

| Slot             | Amount (dry/raw)                                             | Why                                                         |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Legume A**     | 454g (1 lb) dry                                              | Most of the fiber and protein                               |
| **Legume B**     | ~190g (1 cup) dry                                            | More protein, a different texture                           |
| **Grain**        | ~170g (1 cup) dry                                            | Chew, carbs                                                 |
| **Sturdy green** | ~400g                                                        | Volume, micronutrients. It has to survive days of dressing. |
| **Allium**       | ~150g                                                        | Bite                                                        |
| **Extras**       | ~350g total                                                  | Sweetness, color, heat, herbs                               |
| **Dressing**     | 500g 2% Greek yogurt + ~120g acid + mustard + garlic + spice | Holds everything together, and adds protein                 |

**Current build:** black beans, lentils, quinoa, kale, red onion, corn, cilantro, jalapeño, and a lime-cumin yogurt dressing.
**Per serving:** ~627 cal · 41g protein · 22g fiber.

### Swaps

Swap within a slot at the same dry weight and the macros stay within about 10%.

**Legumes** (per 100g dry)

| Legume              | Cal | Protein | Fiber | Notes                                                                   |
| ------------------- | --- | ------- | ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Black beans         | 341 | 22g     | 16g   | The default                                                             |
| Pinto               | 347 | 21g     | 16g   | Creamier                                                                |
| Kidney              | 337 | 23g     | 15g   | Must boil or pressure cook. Never slow cook, because it can stay toxic. |
| Chickpeas           | 378 | 21g     | 12g   | Firm, nutty                                                             |
| Brown/green lentils | 352 | 25g     | 11g   | No soak, 20 min on the stove                                            |
| Split peas          | 341 | 25g     | 26g   | They turn to mush, so they work better in a stew base than a salad      |

**Grains** (per 100g dry)

| Grain          | Cal  | Protein | Fiber | Notes                                         |
| -------------- | ---- | ------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Quinoa         | 368  | 14g     | 7g    | Fast, light                                   |
| Brown rice     | 367  | 8g      | 4g    | The cheapest. Classic beans-and-rice texture. |
| Bulgur         | 342  | 12g     | 18g   | Best fiber. Just soak it in boiling water.    |
| Pearled barley | 352  | 10g     | 16g   | Chewy, holds up well                          |
| Farro          | ~340 | ~13g    | ~11g  | Chewy, nutty                                  |

**Sturdy greens:** kale, collards (sliced thin), shredded Brussels sprouts, green cabbage. Skip lettuce and spinach, because they turn to slime within a day of being dressed.

**Alliums:** red onion, shallot, scallion, pickled onion.

**Extras:** corn, roasted sweet potato, bell pepper, carrot, cucumber (it gets watery, so add it the day you eat it), cherry tomatoes, herbs, chiles.

### Dressing formula

Plain Greek yogurt, acid, mustard, garlic, salt, and one spice direction. No oil. A batch uses 500g of 2% Greek yogurt (~365 cal · 50g protein). Fat-free works too: it saves ~70 cal per batch with the same protein, so use whichever you like the taste of.

**Store the dressing separately and dress each bowl.** Yogurt goes thin and watery when it sits on the base for days. Mixed, the dressing keeps ~5 days in a jar.

Want fat? Half an avocado on the bowl adds ~160 cal · 15g fat · 7g fiber.

| Direction                    | Acid                         | Spice / add-ins                       |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Mexican                      | Lime + red wine vinegar      | Cumin, cilantro, chipotle             |
| Mediterranean (tzatziki-ish) | Lemon                        | Garlic, dill, grated cucumber         |
| Ranch-ish                    | Lemon or apple cider vinegar | Dill, chives, garlic and onion powder |
| Smoky                        | Apple cider vinegar          | Smoked paprika, chipotle              |
| Asian                        | Rice vinegar + lime          | Miso or soy sauce, ginger, scallion   |

### Process

1. **Beans:** Instant Pot with water covering them by 2" plus salt. Black or pinto take 35 min at high pressure with 15 min natural release. Chickpeas take 45. Kidney beans can be pressure cooked, but never slow cooked.
2. **Lentils and grains:** Cook them on the stove at the same time as the beans.
3. **Cool everything** before mixing so the greens don't wilt.
4. **Massage the greens** with salt and a squeeze of lime.
5. **Toss everything except the dressing**, portion into 6 containers, and refrigerate. Mix the dressing in a jar and add it per bowl.

## 2. The Protein

**Target:** ~1 lb cooked per day, which is ~1.35 lb raw. Buy about 4 lb raw every 3 days.

| Protein (per 100g cooked)        | Cal      | Protein | Notes                                              |
| -------------------------------- | -------- | ------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Chicken breast                   | 165      | 31g     | Leanest. Easy to dry out.                          |
| Chicken thigh, boneless skinless | 179      | 25g     | Hard to mess up. Extra calories help you hit 3000. |
| Pork tenderloin / loin           | ~145–170 | ~26–28g | Often the cheapest lean option                     |
| 93% ground turkey                | ~213     | ~27g    | Fastest to cook                                    |
| 90% ground beef                  | ~217     | ~26g    | Flavor. Budget swap.                               |

**Cooking:** sheet pan at 425°F, air fryer, or Instant Pot and shred. Season the whole batch in one direction and match it to the dressing direction.

**Food safety:** cooked meat is good for 3–4 days in the fridge. If the schedule slips, freeze half the batch right after cooking.

## 3. The Ferment

**Default:** curtido, which is fermented cabbage, carrot, onion, jalapeño, and oregano.

### The rule

**Salt = 2% of vegetable weight.** Weigh the vegetables, multiply by 0.02, and that's your salt in grams. Use salt with no iodine and no anti-caking agents.

### Process

1. Shred the vegetables, weigh them, and add the salt.
2. Massage 5–10 min until there's a pool of brine.
3. Pack tightly into a jar with everything under the brine. Leave 2" of headspace.
4. Put on a weight and an airlock lid, and leave it at 65–75°F out of the sun.
5. Taste from day 4. When it's sour enough (usually 5–10 days), refrigerate it.

**Normal:** bubbles, cloudy brine, a sour smell, and a white film on top (kahm yeast, which you just skim off).
**Toss it:** fuzzy mold in any color, pink or slimy vegetables, or a rotten smell.

### Swaps

Anything raw and crunchy works at the same 2% salt:

- **Sauerkraut:** cabbage and salt, optionally caraway
- **Kimchi-ish:** napa cabbage, radish, scallion, garlic, ginger, gochugaru
- **Pickled red onion ferment:** red onion and a few peppercorns
- **Fermented carrots:** carrot sticks, garlic, dill
- **Hot sauce mash:** chiles and garlic, then blended

Don't ferment cooked beans or grains in a jar. They go bad instead of turning sour.

### Gear

- 2–3 wide-mouth half-gallon mason jars
- One wide-mouth airlock lid and one glass weight per jar
- A kitchen scale

## 4. Market Additions

Go to the farmers market, buy whatever looks good, and cook it with one or two ingredients. No recipes. Think in **treatments**, not dishes.

### The universal finish

**Salt it, add acid at the end.** Fat is optional: avocado, a spoon of yogurt, a little hard cheese, or toasted seeds.

### The six treatments

| Treatment             | Method                                                                                                               | Best for                                                                  |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Raw + salt + acid** | Slice, salt, wait 10 min, then a squeeze of acid                                                                     | Tomatoes, cucumbers, raw corn, radishes, fennel                           |
| **Roast hot**         | 425°F or air fryer, salt and a light oil spray (or none in the air fryer), don't crowd, pull when the edges are dark | Squash, carrots, broccoli, cauliflower, beets, eggplant, Brussels sprouts |
| **Char**              | Smoking-hot cast iron or broiler, then salt and lime                                                                 | Peppers, corn, scallions, tomatoes, shishitos                             |
| **Wilt**              | Hot pan, garlic, a splash of water or a little oil, ~3 min                                                           | Greens, zucchini, mushrooms                                               |
| **Cook down**         | Low heat, 30+ min, salt, lid on at first to release juices, until jammy                                              | Soft or overripe tomatoes and peppers                                     |
| **Preserve**          | Quick pickle (1:1 hot vinegar and water, plus salt) or 2% ferment                                                    | Anything you bought too much of                                           |

### How to pick a treatment

- **Watery and tender:** raw, or quick heat
- **Dense or starchy:** roast
- **Leafy or bitter:** wilt
- **Past its prime:** cook down
- **Too much of it:** preserve

### Finishers

Keep a few on hand and grab one: flaky salt, lemon or lime, chile flakes, fresh herbs, a hard cheese, toasted seeds.

### Examples

- **Tomatoes:** salt, a squeeze of lemon
- **Cucumbers:** smash, salt, rice vinegar
- **Peppers:** blister, salt, lime
- **Corn:** char, lime, chile, salt
- **Zucchini:** wilt with garlic, finish with lemon
- **Squash:** roast, then flaky salt and toasted seeds

### At the market

- Buy whatever's piled highest. That's peak season and usually the cheapest.
- Ask the grower how they eat it. You'll almost always get a two-ingredient answer.
- Keep a tub of each treated vegetable in the fridge and put it on top of the bowl next to the ferment.

## The Rhythm

- **Day 1:** Cook the base and the protein. Check the ferment.
- **Days 2–3:** Eat the bowls.
- **Day 4:** Repeat.
- **When the curtido jar is half empty:** Start the next jar.
- **Weekly:** Go to the market, pick a treatment for each vegetable, and fill a few tubs.
- **When you're bored:** Change one slot at a time (a different legume, a different dressing direction, a different ferment) so it stays familiar and you learn what you like.

## Lineage

None of this is new, and that's a good thing. It's where a few well-worn threads meet:

- **Peasant food.** Beans + grains + fermented vegetables is how most of the world has eaten for centuries. Rice and beans with curtido, dal and rice with pickles, kimchi and banchan.
- _**Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat**_ (Samin Nosrat). Cooking from principles instead of recipes.
- _**An Everlasting Meal**_ (Tamar Adler). Cooking from what you have, without recipes.
- _**Six Seasons**_ (Joshua McFadden). Farmers market vegetables, simple treatments.
- _**The Art of Fermentation**_ (Sandor Katz). The ferment side.

## What makes it different

Whole-food and seasonal cooking mostly ignores macros. Macro-driven eating is mostly chicken, rice, and protein bars. This system is **whole-food, seasonal, and fermented, and it's engineered to hit high-protein targets**. That's a much less crowded space.
