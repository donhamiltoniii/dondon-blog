## Overview

Five training days a week across four pieces, plus a daily foot/hip prehab
underlay:

- **Barbell strength** (2 days) — the strength anchor: deadlift, squat, presses,
  pulls.
- **Simple & Sinister** (3 days) — kettlebell swings + Turkish get-ups for hip
  power, grip, unilateral stability, and conditioning. Minimalist and built to
  run alongside the barbell work without interfering.
- **Steel mace** (Sat finisher) — rotational / anti-rotation work the bells and
  barbell don't cover.
- **Easy cardio** (off days) — casual biking for heart and endurance, kept easy
  enough to aid recovery rather than compete with it.
- **Daily prehab** (feet & hips) — plantar-fascia/calf loading for the plantar
  fasciitis, plus hip-flexor mobility and glute/core work for the anterior
  pelvic tilt. A low-fatigue daily layer, not a training day of its own.

It's a forever plan, not a cycle to finish. Nothing here has an end date — the
progressions just keep going.

**Equipment on hand:** pair of 25 lb bells (~11 kg ea), one 20 kg bell,
15 lb steel mace, barbell + dumbbells.

## Weekly Schedule

- **Mon** — S&S
- **Tue** — Barbell A
- **Wed** — S&S
- **Thu** — Off / walk / easy ride
- **Fri** — Barbell B
- **Sat** — S&S + carries
- **Sun** — Off / easy ride

**Daily underlay:** the Feet & Hips prehab block below runs _every day_ (the
mobility/release/activation part). The heavier heel-raise loading piggybacks on
**Tue, Fri, and one S&S day** so it lands every other day without adding sessions.

**Fatigue notes:**

- Wed S&S falls the day after Tue deadlifts. If low back or grip is cooked,
  drop to swings-only (skip get-ups, move them to Sat) or lighten the bell.
  Don't grind it.
- Sat is the one heavier KB day; it gets a full rest day after.
- Each barbell day has a buffer around it so posterior-chain fatigue doesn't
  stack.
- Heel-raise loading is low systemic fatigue but the fascia/tendon still needs
  recovery — alternate days, don't run it back-to-back.

## Easy Cardio

Optional, casual — just balance for all the weight work. Bike on Thu and/or Sun,
30–45 min, **conversational pace** (able to talk the whole time). No intervals,
no long rides, no progression to chase. One or two easy rides a week gives the
full heart/endurance benefit and _helps_ recovery rather than competing with the
lifting. The only rule: keep it easy enough that it never becomes a hard effort.

## Daily Prehab — Feet & Hips

Two targets bolted on as a low-fatigue daily layer:

- **Feet / calves** — plantar-fascia loading + calf strength for the plantar
  fasciitis. Strength is the actual fix here; stretching alone soothes but
  doesn't resolve it.
- **Hips / pelvis** — hip-flexor mobility plus glute/core work to pull the
  pelvis out of anterior tilt and, more importantly, teach it to _hold_ neutral.

Split into a **daily mobility/activation layer** (genuinely every day, ~8–10 min)
and a **loading layer** that runs every other day, because high-load tissue work
needs the off-day to adapt.

### Daily — mobility & activation (~8–10 min, every day)

**Feet:**

- **Plantar release** — roll the arch over a lacrosse/golf ball or a frozen
  water bottle, 1–2 min per foot. Do it _before your first steps in the
  morning_ and again at end of day. The frozen bottle doubles as
  anti-inflammatory.
- **Plantar + calf stretch** — cross the sore foot over the opposite knee and
  pull the toes back toward the shin until the arch stretches, 30s × 3. This
  toe-pull is the plantar-fascia-specific stretch with the best evidence. Then
  a wall calf stretch, knee straight (gastroc) and knee bent (soleus), 30s each.
  Do the toe-pull _before standing up_ in the morning — that first-step pain is
  the fascia being loaded cold.

**Hips:**

- **Half-kneeling hip-flexor stretch** — down leg's glute squeezed, pelvis
  _tucked under (posterior tilt) before_ you shift forward, ribs down, no
  low-back arch. 30–45s each side. The pre-tuck is what aims it at the hip
  flexor instead of the low back — a passive lunge without it mostly misses.
- **Dead bug** — 2×8/side, slow. Low back stays pressed flat into the floor the
  whole rep; if it lifts off, shorten the range. This _is_ the anti-APT skill —
  holding the pelvis down while the limbs move.
- **Glute bridge** — 2×12, pause and squeeze at the top, ribs down, finish with
  a slight posterior tilt rather than cranking the low back into extension.

### Loading — heel raises (every other day, ~6–8 min)

The strength piece, and the part that actually resolves plantar fasciitis rather
than just calming it. Piggyback on **Tue / Fri (barbell) + one S&S day** so it
lands every other day.

- **Setup** — balls of the feet on a step, heels hanging, a rolled towel under
  the toes so they're dorsiflexed. That toe position tensions the plantar fascia
  through the windlass mechanism — it's what makes this plantar-fascia loading
  and not just a calf raise.
- **Tempo** — slow: 3s up, 2s hold at the top, 3s down. Controlled beats heavy.
- **Both knee positions** — straight-knee for the gastroc, then a set knees
  slightly bent for the soleus. The soleus carries most of your standing/walking
  load and is the one most people under-train.

Progression (slow — fascia/tendon adapt on a slower clock than muscle):

- **Start:** two legs, bodyweight, 3×12, toes on towel, slow tempo.
- **When 3×15 is easy:** single leg, 3×8–10.
- **Then load:** hold a dumbbell or the 20 kg bell (goblet or suitcase), or wear
  a loaded pack; keep it 3×8–12.

> Truly _daily_ high-load heel raises will outrun recovery — the irritated
> tissue in plantar fasciitis needs the off-day to adapt, so the **loading** is
> every other day. The mobility/release/activation layer is the genuinely daily
> part. If morning first-step pain is the main symptom, the single
> highest-value habit is the toe-pull stretch + arch roll _before_ your feet hit
> the floor.

> **Under the bar:** don't chase an active posterior tuck on a heavy
> squat/deadlift — you'll lose bracing. Cue _ribs down into a neutral pelvis_
> there. Save the deliberate posterior-tilt work for the bridges, dead bugs, and
> stretches, where you can actually train the position.

**Already covered:** the goblet squats, hip hinges, and get-ups you're doing hit
much of the anti-APT work already — glute-driven hinge, tall overhead position,
braced core. The hip-flexor stretch and dead bug are the missing links, not a
whole new program.

> Both plantar fasciitis and postural APT respond to _consistency over
> intensity_ — small daily doses beat occasional hard sessions. If foot pain is
> sharp, worsening, or unchanged after 6–8 weeks of loading, get it looked at;
> stubborn heel pain isn't always fasciitis.

## Barbell Days

**Barbell A / B** — same session both days:

1. Pull-ups / dips supersets — 5×3, assisted, 45 lb
2. Deadlifts — 3×3, 255–285, progressive
3. Squats — 3×3, 205–225, progressive
4. Chest press / bent-over rows supersets — 3×5, 55 lb dumbbells (each hand)

> **Plateau watch:** 3×3 on squat/deadlift is low volume. If the barbell
> numbers stall for ~4–8 weeks, the fix is on the barbell side — add a back-off
> set or a 4th working set on squat/deadlift. Adding kettlebell volume won't
> break a barbell plateau.

## Kettlebell Days (S&S)

**Warmup** — one round of each, using a 25 lb bell:

- **Goblet squats** — 1×10. Hold the bell at your chest, elbows inside your
  knees at the bottom, sit straight down between your hips. Pause a beat at the
  bottom; this is opening the hips more than building strength.
- **Hip hinges** — 1×10. Bell held at the chest or hanging in both hands. Push
  the hips _back_ (not down), soft knees, feel the hamstrings load, stand tall
  by squeezing the glutes. This grooves the swing pattern — do it deliberately.
- **Halos** — 5 each direction. Bell held by the horns, upside-down, circled
  slowly around the head. Keep the ribs down and core tight so it's the
  shoulders moving, not the low back arching.

**Work:**

- **One-hand swings** — 10×10, alternating hands each set (100 total). Hinge and
  snap the hips to float the bell to chest height — it's a hip drive, not a
  front raise, so the arm stays relaxed. Bell floats up, gravity brings it back,
  you hinge to catch it. Set the bell down and switch hands between sets. Rest
  as needed early; the long-term goal is all 100 in ~5 min.
- **Turkish get-ups** — 10 total, 1 rep per set alternating sides (5 each). One
  full rep = floor all the way to standing and back down, one bell pressed
  overhead the whole time. Move through each position slowly and pause — this is
  a skill lift, not a burner. Take as long as you need per rep; there's no tempo
  to chase. A full get-up runs ~30–45 sec each side when done right.

That's the whole session on Mon and Wed. Saturday adds carries and the mace
finisher below.

## Saturday Add-Ons

**Carries** (after swings/get-ups): 3–4 rounds farmer or suitcase carry, ~40 yd
each. Use the 25 lb pair for farmer, single bell for suitcase. Suitcase (offset,
single bell) is anti-lateral-flexion core work that carries into squat/deadlift
bracing.

**Mace finisher** (~5–8 min, after carries): 15 lb.

- **360s** — full circle behind the head and back. Start 5–10 per direction,
  strict. Learn this first; the pass behind the head is the whole skill.
- **10-to-2s** — shorter pendulum arc, gentler on the shoulder. Build volume
  here once 360s are controlled.

> The mace feels much heavier than 15 lb because the load sits way out past
> your hands. Learn the 360 slow and film it from _behind_. Rushing or going
> heavy early is how the shoulder gets tweaked.

## Loading

Swing/get-up loads don't map to barbell numbers — start conservative. The
_stimulus is the 100-swing volume, not the weight._ Ramp the bell over weeks,
not days.

### Swings

- **Start:** 20 kg
- **Next:** 24 kg
- **Simple standard (men):** 32 kg
- **Long-game:** 32 kg+

### Get-ups

- **Start:** 25 lb / 11 kg
- **Next:** 20 kg
- **Simple standard (men):** —
- **Long-game:** 32 kg

### Mace 360s

- **Start:** 15 lb
- **Next:** —
- **Simple standard (men):** —
- **Long-game:** 20–25 lb

### Heel raises (plantar-fascia loading)

- **Start:** two-leg bodyweight, 3×12, toes on towel, slow tempo
- **Next:** single-leg bodyweight, 3×8–10
- **Simple standard:** single-leg + light load (DB / 20 kg bell), 3×8–12
- **Long-game:** single-leg, heavier load, both knee positions, pain-free

> **Swings:** 20 kg is the only bell in range — use it, but if 10×10 one-hand
> feels ragged, do _two-hand_ swings at 20 kg until the hinge is clean, then
> move to one-hand. Don't let the bell turn the swing into a squat.
>
> **Get-ups:** start with a 25 lb bell from the pair, one per session. Get-ups
> punish ego — a bell you can't control overhead is how shoulders get hurt.
> Under-load on purpose. Next jump is the 20 kg once both sides are solid.
>
> **Double work (later):** the 25 lb pair unlocks double swings, double front
> squats, and matched farmer carries once the basics are automatic.
>
> **Heel raises:** progress reps before load, and single-leg before either.
> Add weight only when the last set is clean and pain-free — plantar tissue
> punishes rushed loading worse than a bell does.
